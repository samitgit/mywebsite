I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computational & Applied Mathematics (Mathematics of Data Science) in the Department of Mathematics and Statistics at Old Dominion University. My research develops theoretically grounded, scalable algorithms for statistical estimation and scientific machine learning.

My dissertation, On the Maximum Trimmed Likelihood Estimator, is advised by Dr. Michael Pokojovy. I also work on Gaussian-process surrogates and operator learning for high-dimensional PDEs, with applications to computational finance.

I completed my M.S. in Data Science at ODU (2024), and my B.S. in Mathematics and M.S. in Applied Mathematics at the University of Dhaka, Bangladesh. I am happy to talk about research or collaboration; please email me at s2ghosh@odu.edu.

Research

Robust statistics and optimization

Trimmed likelihood estimation and least trimmed squares, with convergence-rate analysis and first-order optimization methods for penalized trimmed estimators under data contamination. Related interests include large-scale and convex optimization, including Frank–Wolfe methods.

Background reading: short introduction to Frank–Wolfe, soft margin boosting, Deep Frank–Wolfe.

Scientific machine learning

Supervised and unsupervised learning, and Gaussian-process-based surrogates (EPGP) and operator learning for high-dimensional PDEs. Applications include option pricing in computational finance and comparisons against finite-element methods.

Background reading: deep parametric PDE method, differential NNs for Black–Scholes, NN learning of Black–Scholes.

Broader interests

High-dimensional modeling and variable selection, model selection, and scalable algorithms for data-driven scientific discovery. I am also interested in machine learning for fraud detection (for example, this Scientific Reports article).
